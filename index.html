Fengze Guo

My research interests center on understanding how language models learn and compute, and how these processes give rise to their capabilities, generalization, and failures.

  • Learning and Internal Computation: How do language models acquire and organize linguistic and world knowledge during training? How do representations and computational mechanisms develop, and what shapes what models ultimately learn?
  • Generalization and Model Behavior: How do learned representations and computations translate into behavior? When do new capabilities emerge, and how robustly do they generalize to novel, complex, and interactive settings?
  • Failures and Reliability: When and why do language models fail, particularly in settings that require reasoning, integrating external information, or adapting beyond familiar distributions? How are failures such as hallucination, factual errors, and unreliable reasoning connected to how models learn, represent, and use information, and how can this understanding help improve reliability and robust generalization?

Research

Selected research projects on language models, interpretability, multilingual NLP, and model evaluation.

Layer-wise Lexical Accessibility in Language Models: A Plain Logit Lens Study across English and Chinese
Interpretability Logit Lens Language Models

I use the plain Logit Lens to track when predefined lexical targets become accessible across model layers, comparing English and Chinese, semantic groups, and Qwen3 model scales. I also study semantically related alternatives and cases where an accessible gold target is later replaced by the final prediction.

SemEval-2026 Task 9: Multilingual Online Polarization Detection
Multilingual NLP Classification Model Evaluation

I developed and evaluated multilingual classifiers across 22 languages, studying cross-language and label variation, severe class imbalance, calibration, and heterogeneous ensembling.

Controlled Schema Anonymization in Text-to-SQL
Text-to-SQL Controlled Evaluation Schema Semantics

I designed controlled schema-anonymization experiments to separate reliance on lexical schema semantics from relational structure, evaluating how progressive information removal affects different SQL query types.
