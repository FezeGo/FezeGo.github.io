Fengze Guo

My research interests center on three connected questions: how language models learn, how they reason, and how to make them more reliable. I am also interested in human-centered AI and long-horizon human–AI collaboration.

  • Learning: How models acquire, organize, and represent knowledge across training.
  • Reasoning: How internal representations and computations support reasoning and problem solving.
  • Reliability: Why models fail, including hallucination and unreliable reasoning, and how to improve robustness.

Research

Selected research projects on language models, interpretability, multilingual NLP, and model evaluation.

Layer-wise Lexical Accessibility in Language Models
Interpretability Language Models Multilingual NLP

I study when predefined target tokens become accessible across model layers using the plain Logit Lens, with comparisons across English and Chinese, semantic groups, and model scales. I also analyze cases in which an accessible target is later replaced by a different final prediction.

Multilingual Polarization Detection
Multilingual NLP Classification Robustness

I worked on multilingual text classification across 22 languages, focusing on model behavior under sparse and distribution-shifted conditions.

Schema Anonymization in Text-to-SQL
Text-to-SQL LLM Evaluation Schema Semantics

I investigated how progressively removing lexical information from database schemas affects LLM-based Text-to-SQL performance, with controlled comparisons across joins, aggregation, nested queries, and other query types.
